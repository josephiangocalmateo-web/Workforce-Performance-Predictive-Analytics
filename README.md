# Corporate Workforce Performance & Predictive Output Analytics
**Tools Used:** Microsoft Excel (Power Query, Forecasting Models, Correlation Analysis, Pivot Tables)

## 💡 1. The Business Problem & Objectives
*   **Context:** This project analyzes a 1,000-row human capital dataset tracking employee performance scores, headcount density, and project metrics across 5 major corporate departments (Finance, HR, IT, Operations, Sales) over a 9-year historical timeline (2016–2024).
*   **Problem Statement:** The organization has demonstrated stable but moderate output, carrying an overall average performance score of 3.02 out of 5. Performance has become stagnant. To break this plateau, leadership needs to mathematically isolate the true drivers of employee productivity and forecast performance trends to the year 2030.
*   **Objectives:** 
    1. Conduct statistical correlation tests to identify whether project volume or headcount density drives overall performance scores.
    2. Analyze departmental pay structures to evaluate their impact on workplace productivity.
    3. Deploy time-series predictive modeling to forecast organizational performance baselines over the next 5 years.

## 🔍 2. Data Engineering & Analytics Methodology
*   **Workforce Ingestion:** Standardized and format-locked a 1,000-employee data architecture managing a cumulative total workload of 24,989 projects across the 9-year period.
*   **Bivariate Correlation Analysis:** Calculated Pearson Correlation Coefficients (\(r\)) to identify structural performance drivers, isolating a weak positive relationship between Project Volume and Performance (\(r = 0.26\)) but an exceptionally strong positive relationship between Staff Headcount and Performance (\(r = 0.69\)).
*   **Time-Series Predictive Forecasting:** Programmed an exponential smoothing forecast model with a 95% confidence interval bound to project performance stagnancy behaviors from 2025 through 2030.

*   **📈 The Headcount Dependency Breakthrough:** Bivariate analysis proves that employee counts heavily dictate success. 2018 and 2021 marked our historical peak performance years. These peak years correspond directly to our highest employee headcounts (120 and 129 employees), proving that greater workforce capacity directly prevents burnout, enhances engagement, and lifts the overall corporate average performance score.
*   **🏆 Departmental Productivity Top Performer:** Talent distribution across departments is remarkably even (ranging strictly between 19% and 21%). However, the Finance department heavily leads the organization in overall productivity, completing 5,415 projects while simultaneously achieving the highest average performance score of 3.17 out of 5. 
*   **💰 The Compensation-to-Performance Gap:** Interestingly, HR commands the highest average monthly salary floor at ₱75,066.02, yet maintains an average performance score of only 3.01. Conversely, the Finance department carries a lower monthly average salary (₱72,091.57) but yields significantly higher performance (3.17), signaling a clear gap in pay-to-performance alignment.
*   **🔮 2030 Stagnancy Predictive Forecast:** Predictive modeling shows that without operational intervention, organizational performance scores will remain entirely stagnant, hovering tightly between a 2.5 and 3.0 line through the year 2030. 

## 🚀 4. Strategic Recommendations
1. **Increase Strategic Headcount & Project Volume:** Because a strong positive relationship (\(r = 0.69\)) exists between employee count and performance, leadership must view hiring not as a cost, but as a performance driver. Strategically increase target headcounts during intensive quarters to expand output capacity and sustain motivation.
2. **Replicate the Finance Department Operational Model:** Conduct an immediate deep-dive audit into the Finance team’s structural workflows, onboarding methods, and task allocation strategies. Use their high-volume success (5,415 projects / 3.17 score) as the corporate blueprint to uplift underperforming teams like Sales (2.95) and Operations (2.96).
3. **Deconstruct Historical Success Factors (2018 & 2021):** Launch a focused retrospective analysis into the exact operational shifts, training paths, and management changes that occurred during the 2018 and 2021 peak years to replicate those success conditions today.
4. **Targeted Upskilling & Training Programs:** Implement continuous growth paths, professional seminars, and expanded industry exposure company-wide to directly push the corporate performance baseline beyond its projected 3.02 ceiling.
